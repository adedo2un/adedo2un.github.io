THE PROBLEM

Global platforms weren't built for this context

Coursera, Udemy, and edX were designed for users in the global north — assuming reliable broadband, credit cards, and culturally neutral content. For African university students, those assumptions create real, daily barriers.

This wasn't a surface-level design problem. It required rethinking infrastructure, payment, content curation, and cultural identity at every layer of the product.

01 📶
Unstable connectivity
68% of Nigerian students report frequent connectivity drops that interrupt streaming. One dropped connection can undo hours of learning progress.
02 💸
Prohibitive data costs
Mobile data costs 3–8% of monthly income for the average student. A single HD lecture can consume an entire day's data budget.
03 🗺️
No African context
Global curricula use American and European case studies exclusively. Students want instructors who look like them, teaching examples they recognise.
04 💳
Broken payment flows
Credit card penetration is under 5% for most African students. Most checkout flows fail entirely — not because students can't pay, but because the design doesn't support how they do.

From research to resolution — every step

Define & Frame
Affinity mapping of 140 research notes into 6 core themes. Problem statement: "African university students need a way to access quality, relevant education that works within real infrastructure constraints — because today's platforms were built for someone else."
WEEK 4
Ideate & Prioritise
Crazy 8s sketching session. 60+ solution ideas reduced to 12 through impact/effort mapping. Three big bets emerged: offline-first architecture, African content curation badge, and mobile money checkout. Information architecture finalised across 8 core screens.
💡
✏️
WEEK 5–6
Prototype
Low-fidelity wireframes for 8 screens → mid-fidelity component review → high-fidelity Figma prototype with full interaction states. Design system built with 60+ components covering typography, colour, spacing, states, and accessibility annotations.
WEEK 7–8
Test, Iterate & Ship
3 rounds of moderated usability testing with 18 participants. SUS score rose from 64 (Round 1) to 87 (Round 3). Major iterations: simplified onboarding from 5 screens to 3, reordered checkout flow, increased touch target sizes for low-end devices. Final prototype delivered for development handoff.

KEY DESIGN DECISIONS

Three decisions that
changed everything

01
Offline-first, always
Every lesson downloads in two tiers — Standard (60% less data) and HD. Progress syncs automatically when connection returns. The entire product was architected around the assumption of no internet, not the assumption of reliable internet. This resolved the single biggest reason students abandoned existing platforms.
↓ 68% — drop-off from connectivity loss
02
The "Made in Africa" badge
Courses taught by African instructors using African case studies display a distinctive cultural badge. It took two hours to design and ship. In A/B testing, badged courses achieved 2.3× higher enrolment than identical unbadged courses. Students reported feeling "seen" and "trusting the content more." Small signal, massive meaning.
2.3× — enrolment on badged courses
03
3-tap mobile money checkout
I rebuilt the entire payment flow around how African students actually pay — Paystack, Flutterwave, MTN Mobile Money, Airtel Money. No credit card required. The flow is 3 taps from course page to receipt. Payment drop-off, which averaged 74% on competitor platforms, fell to 18% in testing.
↓ 56pt — payment drop-off reduction

REFLECTIONS

What this project
taught me

EduAfrika was the project that changed how I think about design. Not the prettiest, not the most technically complex — but the most honest.

01
Context is the brief
The most important design decisions on this project had nothing to do with visual style. Offline-first wasn't a feature — it was the entire product philosophy. You can't design well for a context you haven't lived in, which is why research isn't optional.
02
Small signals carry enormous weight
The "Made in Africa" badge was a 2-hour design decision. It moved enrolment by 2.3×. Students said they felt "seen" and "trusted the content more." Cultural representation in product design isn't just ethical — it's measurably effective.
03
Real users will always surprise you
My first onboarding was 5 screens. I thought it built enough context. Testing showed 3 was the hard limit before drop-off. I had assumed. They corrected me. The discipline is learning to hold your assumptions lightly until someone real sits in front of your prototype.
